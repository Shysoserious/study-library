Time Value of Money
• Money has a time value.
• Which would you prefer: $10,000 today or $10,000 next year? 
–
Obviously, $10,000 today!
–
You can take $10,000 now, put the money in the bank that pays 2% interest per year, which 
will be $10,200 one year from now. 
–
In other words, one dollar today is worth more than one dollar tomorrow.
• You already recognize that there is TIME VALUE OF MONEY!!
• TIME allows you the opportunity to postpone consumption and 
earn INTEREST.

Interpretation of PV and FV
• PV and FV help us establish equivalence between cash now and cash in the future.
–
Future Value is the value of a present amount of money at some future time, at a given interest rate.
–
Present Value is the value of a future amount of money at present time, at a given interest rate.
• With a given interest rate, we can use PV and FV to compare cash flow at different points in time.
–
Example: If the interest rate is 3%, will you prefer 1,000 today or 1,145.27 in five years?
–
FV of 1,000 = 1,000 ∗1 + 3% 5 = 1,159.27. 
–

Lecture 4: Time Value of Money
Annual Percentage Rate (APR)
• Interest rate is required by law to be quoted in annual frequency, which is called 
annual percentage rate (APR)
• When APR is stated, it always entails information on number of interest paying 
periods per year (m)
–
APR = per period rate * the number of periods per year (m)
–
Therefore, per period rate = APR / number of periods per year (m)
•
Per-period rate is also called effective rate or effective rate per period
–
Example:
•
APR of 12% with monthly compounding →m = 12; per period (monthly) rate = 12%/12 = 1% 
•
APR of 12% with quarterly compounding →m = 4; per period (quarterly) rate = 12%/4 = 3% 
•
APR of 12% with semiannual compounding →m = 2; per period (semiannual) rate = 12%/2 = 6%

Effective Annual Rate (EAR)
• Effective annual rate (EAR): the actual interest rate during the year, after accounting for compounding
• APR is the quoted interest rate (on paper). EAR is the actual interest rate (you pay).
• You can always convert APR to EAR using the compounding frequency. 
• Example: Assume that you borrow $100. What would be the actual amount you pay annually? 
–
If compounded annually, annual interest for a $100 debt is $18.99
–
If compounded semiannually, annual interest is $100*[(1+0.1899/2)2 -1]= $19.89
–
If compounded monthly, annual interest is $100*[ (1+0.1899/12)12 -1]= $20.73
–
If compounded daily, annual interest is $100*[ (1+0.1899/365)365 -1]= $20.91
