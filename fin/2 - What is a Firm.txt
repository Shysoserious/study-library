Lecture 4: What is a Firm
Limited Partnership (LP)
In LP, there are two types of partners:
• General partner (GP) : at least one who bears unlimited liability and runs the 
business
• Limited partners (LP): other partners who bear limited liability and usually do not 
run the business
• Makes it easier to raise capital from investors!
• Under limited liability, you only lose what you’ve invested in the worst case scenario
• The typical form used by money managers (such as venture capital and private equity)

Lecture 4: What is a Firm
Financial Management Goals and CSR
• Starting from 1950s, and especially after 2000, people are rethinking whether the 
goal should be only to maximize shareholders’ value. 
–
Typically, firm value increase = the whole society benefits.   
–
It becomes a problem when firm value increase →some stakeholders (especially the weak) suffers. 
•
Stakeholders that receive main attention includes employees, consumers, and the environment.
• CSR (Corporate Social Responsibility): firms should operate ethically and contribute 
positively to the society while pursuing economics objectives.
–
Essentially, there should be a boundary on things that the manager can do when trying to 
maximize the firm value. 
–
Laws may not be able to cover every aspect.

Lecture 4: What is a Firm
Agency Problems are Prevalent at All Levels! (not just CEOs)
• Consider a simple scenario: our firm can produce a good with cost=$100, a 
customer C is willing to pay $200 for the good.
–
Kickback/bribery: The customer can offer our sales representative a rebate. That is, if she 
charges $1 less, she gets $0.5 directly from C.
–
Embezzlement: Our CEO sets up a new firm that purchases the good for $101 from our firm 
and sells to C for $200.
–
Information Leakage: Our worker tells C that our production cost is only $100, so C is only 
willing to pay $105 now.
–
Destructive competition: Our sales representative for a different region may want to steal the 
deal by offering a lower price.
“In a firm, one third of people do work, one third do nothing, and one third prevents the first third from 
doing anything.”

Ways to Alleviate the Agency Problem
• Managerial compensation
–
Incentives can be used to align management and stockholder interests
–
Commonly used incentives include bonus, stocks, and options
• Monitoring (watch over the managers)
–
Internal: limit the power of each person with rules and regulations
–

–
Shareholders: sell shares, use media, sue, vote
–
Lenders (banks, etc.): debt covenants, refuse to renew loan
• Outside control
–
Threat of takeover
–
Labor supply (workers refuse to work for a firm with bad reputation)
