Venture Capital (VC) Financing
• What: a type of financing that individual investors or financial institutions provide to 
small, early-stage, emerging firms.
• Goal: capture very high return from the very few companies that succeed, while most 
of the other companies fail.
• Securities used:
–
Equity
–
Convertible debt: can be converted to a set number of shares of a stock at certain dates 
•
Protected on the downside, can capture the upside
•
Example: Peter Thiel gave Facebook $500,000 for a convertible debt in seed round, which could be 
exchanged into 10.2% of the company

Initial Public Offering (IPO)

–
By going public, the shares can now be traded on an public 
exchange (e.g., NYSE, Shenzhen Stock exchange)
–
Anyone (the general public) can invest in this company
–
The company is subjected to more stringent disclosure rules
• Why:
–
To have better access to financing
–
The value of the equity is typically higher
•
Why? (1) Increased demand. (2) Increased liquidity.
•
With higher valuation: 
•
shares can be used to award employees; 
•
shares can be used to buy another company
–
Better brand recognition

Lecture 3: Raising Capital
Why is There Persistent Underpricing?
• On average, it’s impossible for there to be overpricing—there will be no buyers for 
IPO shares. Investing in IPO markets is risky, and underpricing compensates buyers 
for the risks.
–
Underpricing is a natural phenomenon!
• Another question: Is the underpricing too much? 
–
Probably true for China: 80% for the entire sample period[ref]
–
Reason: Until 2023, regulation set upper bounds on IPO prices (~23 times 
earnings), which was too rigid for many firms.

Lecture 3: Raising Capital
Seasoned Equity Offering
• Additional equity issuance after IPO
–
Example: Facebook issued 27M new shares after it was included into the S&P 
500.[1]
• A stylized finding about SEO
–
Short term impact: stock price usually drops by about 3% upon SEO 
announcements.
–
Why? 
(1)Increased supply. 
(2)Adverse selection: investors would think that the manager would only issue shares if he/she thinks 
the stock is overpriced.
–
Long term: firms that have undergone SEO underperform peer firms in the next 5 
years.[2]
