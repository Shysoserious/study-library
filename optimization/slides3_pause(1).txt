Why Feature Scaling Matters
Different features can have different units: RMB, years, or binary values.
Why this matters geometrically:
▶Rescaling a coordinate changes distances and angles in feature space.
▶A feature with a much larger numerical scale can dominate the
learned boundary.
Example:
▶Salary may be around 100000 RMB, while years in job may be
between 0 and 10.
▶Without scaling, salary differences can overwhelm the geometry.
▶A common fix is normalization or standardization before training.
Takeaway: feature scaling changes the shape of the point cloud and can
change the learned boundary.

Geometry and Optimization: Putting It Together
Geometry:
▶The classifier maps x to a label using the sign of u(x). The
hyperplane w⊤x + b = 0 separates its prediction regions.
▶Margin boundaries: w⊤x + b = ±1.
▶For w̸ = 0, full margin width is 2/∥w∥; each half is 1/∥w∥.
Optimization:
▶Hard margin: maximize width, equivalently minimize 1
2∥w∥2 subject
to all separation constraints.
▶Soft margin (λ > 0): balance the norm penalty and hinge losses;
auxiliary variables give an equivalent QP.
▶Without the norm penalty (λ = 0): hinge-loss minimization has an
equivalent LP formulation.
Intuition: seek a wide margin while penalizing violations, including
correctly classified points inside their margin.

What Does “Linear” Mean?
A function is linear in the decision variables if it has the form
a1x1 + a2x2 + · · · + anxn,

Standard Form of LP: Why Often m < n?
Standard form:
min
x∈Rn c⊤x
s.t.
Ax = b, x ≥0.
Why do we often have m < n?
▶Each independent equality reduces the degrees of freedom.
▶If m = n and A is invertible, Ax = b has the unique solution
x = A−1b, which may violate x ≥0.
▶To have choices to optimize over, we need fewer independent
equalities than variables: rank(A) < n. For independent rows, this
means m < n.
Geometric insight: when consistent, Ax = b is a flat “sheet” (an affine
subspace). Adding x ≥0 cuts it to a polyhedral feasible set.
