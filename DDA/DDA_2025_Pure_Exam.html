Question 3 — Logistic Regression with Polynomial Features (15 points)

Suppose you are given a binary classification task where each input $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$, and the feature vector $\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_i)$ includes all monomials up to degree 2. That is, for $\mathbf{x}_i = (x_{i1}, x_{i2})$, we define

$$\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_i) = (1,\; x_{i1},\; x_{i2},\; x_{i1}^2,\; x_{i2}^2,\; x_{i1}x_{i2})^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6.$$

The model is

$$P(y_i = 1 \mid \mathbf{x}_i, \boldsymbol{\theta}) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\boldsymbol{\theta}^\top \boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_i)}},$$

where $y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ and $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^6$ are the model parameters.

(a) (4 points)

Derive the log-likelihood function $\ell(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ for $n$ independent training examples $\{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$. Write $\ell(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ explicitly in terms of $\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x}_i)$ and $\boldsymbol{\theta}$.

(b) (4 points)

Is the negative log-likelihood function $-\ell(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ convex in $\boldsymbol{\theta}$? Explain carefully.

(c) (5 points)

Suppose you want to find $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ by gradient descent. Write the update rule using a learning rate $\eta > 0$. How does stochastic gradient descent modify the update rule?

(d) (2 points)

Question 7 — MLE + Convex Optimization (19 points)

A seller plans to sell a single type of product and faces uncertain demand $D$. To meet the demand, the seller can produce units in two ways:

  • Pre-production: Before sales begin, the seller can produce any number of units at a cost of $5 per unit. However, any unsold pre-produced units will have no salvage value (i.e., zero residual value).
  • Rush production: After observing the realized demand $D$, the seller can rush produce additional units if needed, but at a higher cost of $9 per unit.

The demand $D$ can be satisfied either by pre-produced units or rush-produced units, where each unit sold generates a revenue of $p > 9$. Pre-production offers a lower cost but carries the risk of overproducing, while rush production is more flexible but incurs a higher cost. The seller must determine the optimal pre-production quantity before observing the realized demand, with the objective of maximizing expected profit.

(a) (3 points)

After observing the realized demand $D$, how many units should the seller rush produce? Prove that the optimal number of rush-produced units is $(D - Q)^+$, where $x^+ = \max(x, 0)$. Explain your reasoning.

(b) (3 points)

Show that at the optimal decision, the seller's total sales revenue (i.e., the total amount collected from selling products, excluding any production costs) is $p E[D]$. That is, all demand will be satisfied.

(c) (4 points)

Suppose the random demand $D$ follows a uniform distribution over $[\theta, \theta^2]$, where $\theta$ is an unknown number. You are given 5 observed samples: $\{12, 15, 13, 16, 14\}$. Find the MLE of $\theta$. Explain your steps clearly.

(d) (6 points)

Now assume that $D$ follows a uniform distribution over $[\theta, \theta^2]$ with $\theta$ equal to the MLE you found in part (c). Prove that the seller's optimal pre-production quantity is $Q^* = 28/3$, if allowing $Q$ to be non-integer. Explain your calculations.

(e) (3 points)

If $Q$ must be an integer (the rush productions can still be fractional), what is the optimal decision of $Q$? Briefly explain your answer.
